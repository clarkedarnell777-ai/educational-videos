# Social publishing package — Your Pay Went Up. Your Buying Power Didn't.

Figures: BLS Real Earnings and Consumer Price Index, September 2026 (both released Oct 14, 2026). Average hourly earnings for all private employees rose 3.0% from Sep 2025 to Sep 2026; CPI-U rose 3.7%; real average hourly earnings fell 0.7%. The $60,000 salary example is illustrative. October CPI and real earnings are due Nov 10, 2026 — check whether they change the picture before posting.

## YouTube Shorts
**Title:** Your Pay Went Up 3%. Prices Went Up 3.7%. Here's What That Means.

**Description:**
Average hourly pay rose 3.0% over the year to September 2026, but consumer prices rose 3.7%, so real hourly pay fell 0.7% (BLS). Nominal pay is the number on your paycheck; real pay is what it buys. Illustrative math: on a $60,000 salary, a 3% raise adds $1,800, but keeping pace with 3.7% inflation took $2,220, a $420 shortfall in buying power (before taxes).

Sources: BLS Real Earnings, Sep 2026 (Oct 14, 2026); BLS CPI, Sep 2026 (Oct 14, 2026).
